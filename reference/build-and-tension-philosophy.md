## Key principles surfaced (skim for integration into skill)

1. **The two-factor formula:** every YouTube script is a function of **build intention + tension**. *"What's the one point You're Building towards and how do you create tension along the way."* Build = the one point. Tension = the technique used to keep them watching until you reveal it. Strength of both determines whether the script is average / great / killer.
2. **Outline-first, always.** *"Tactics flow from a superior position"* (Bobby Fisher). Don't write a script until you've written an outline: **Intro → Climax → Resolution**. Three things. Almost every YouTuber skips this. Reinforces Kyle's "structure is #1" rule and Iman's Step 4 (manual outline before Claude).
3. **The killer-outline test:** before you start scripting, you should be able to answer (a) what's the intro hook, (b) what's the one climactic point you're building toward, (c) how does the story resolve. If you can't say it in one sentence each, the outline isn't done.
4. **Two-climax problem.** If your outline has two climaxes ("explain why" + "validate the leaver"), the script will lose tension at the seam between them. Force them into ONE climax by collapsing both into a single concept (in the worked example: "closure through understanding"). Connects directly to Kyle's *"Setup-Framework Alignment"* rule from 2026-05-24 coaching-feedback.
5. **Ruthless cutting.** *"If you're not taking out 10% you're not trying very hard."* Every line should build from intro to climax. If it doesn't contribute, cut it. Reinforces the skill's R11 (trim manually).
6. **Open and close loops continuously.** *"Anytime I build anticipation for what's to come next, I open a loop. I create tension in my video because the viewer just like has to close that loop. And this constant opening and closing of loops is just the essence of captivating writing."* Aligns with packaging Section open/close loops rule, but extends it across the WHOLE script, not just the packaging section.
7. **Emotional range — surf the wave, don't flatline.** *"Rather than writing your viewers emotions Flatline try to peek up and down the full spectrum of emotion. Make them scared, make them laugh, make them angry, relieved, happy, sad, crushed, nervous, joyful."* NEW principle not currently captured in the skill's reference files. Worth adding to copywriting-structures.md as a body-point design rule.
8. **The invisible element: fall in love with the project.** *"The YouTube videos we really enjoy, it's the ones where the Creator fell in love making it."* Vibe-y, but real. If the writer is bored, the viewer will be bored. If the writer struggled and didn't enjoy it, the script reads forced.

# Problem-Mechanism Intro — Teaching + Scaffold

How to open a YouTube video by leading with the **mechanism of the problem** before the mechanism of the solution. Pattern taught by Luis (Educate.io coach) on the 2026-05-21 coaching call. Use this whenever the video's ICP is problem-aware (knows they're stuck, doesn't yet know why).

> *Coaching-call rule of thumb (Luis): "I would lead with mechanism problem... then once you review what the problem is, then the solution becomes clear."*

---

## 1. The principle (Luis, in his own words)

Luis: *"All the problems that you have, they're actually not a problem. They are a symptom of an underlying problem that I call X Y and Z, and then you come up with a name for it... I would introduce that pretty quickly, I would just open that loop. And then I'd go into authority... You're going into the mechanism of the solution, right, the SCALE equation. That would be the mechanism of the solution. But instead of going straight into the mechanism of the solution, I would first introduce the mechanism of the problem... Your solution mechanism is the reason why everything they tried before didn't work, and why this one would be different."*

Two moves are baked into that:

1. **Symptom → underlying cause.** The viewer thinks they have many problems (low conversion, dead content, bad pricing). You reframe all of them as symptoms of ONE underlying problem and you give that underlying problem a coined name.
2. **Solution-as-counter-to-problem.** Later, when you introduce your solution mechanism, you explicitly say: *everything you've tried before patched a symptom; this one addresses the named problem at the root.*

Together they make the solution feel inevitable instead of "another framework."

Reference video Luis cited as a clean example of problem-mechanism done well: **Ryan Deiss's *"why you struggle to scale your business"* VSL/intro.**

---

## 2. When to use it — the audience-sophistication rule

Luis: *"It depends on mainly the sophistication that they have in the market. For you, they're more sophisticated people, right. And they know there's trouble with a certain type of problem so you can have a problem-driven copy here. Mine doesn't even know like problem-solution. So I need to lead with opportunity, which is even before the problem."*

| Audience awareness | Lead with | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem-aware** (feels the pain, doesn't know the cause) | **Problem mechanism** | Naming the cause is the aha. |
| **Solution-aware** (knows the type of solution exists, comparing options) | **Problem mechanism, sharpened** | Differentiate by naming a problem competitors miss. |
| **Most-aware** (knows you, deciding) | **Solution mechanism or offer** | Skip the diagnosis. |
| **Unaware** (doesn't yet know they have this problem) | **Opportunity first, then problem** | Sell the upside before the diagnosis. Problem-first here will lose them. |

**The honest test before you script:** would your ICP say in a DM, *"I'm stuck at X and I don't know why"*? If yes, problem-aware → use this scaffold. If they'd instead say *"I didn't even know X was possible"* → don't; use opportunity-first.

## 3. The 5-part scaffold

After validating the click and stating authority (the first ~30 seconds, per Luis), the problem-mechanism block runs:

### a. The wrong belief
Name what they currently think the problem is. Often it's "I'm not doing enough."
> *"Most [ICP] think the reason they're stuck at [outcome] is [wrong cause]. Not enough X. Not enough Y. Not enough Z."*

### b. The reframe
Invert it. Make the wrong belief part of the problem.
> *"It's the opposite. You're stuck because [the actual underlying dynamic]."*

### c. Name + label the underlying problem
State the structural truth in one sentence. Then coin the name. *This is the move Luis is asking for — don't skip it.* The name is the loop hook; it's what the viewer remembers and what every later loop hangs on.
> *"Here's the mechanism. [One-sentence structural truth.] I call this **[Named Problem]**."*

### d. Symptom map
List 3–5 surface problems the viewer is currently chasing and collapse each one into the named problem. This is where the aha happens — they realise they don't have five separate problems, they have one.
> *"Your X — that's [the named problem]. Your Y — same thing. Your Z — same thing again."*

### e. Open the loop
Tease a specific aspect of the named problem the viewer would not guess. Do not close it here. Close it later in the body or in the retention loop at the end of the intro.
> *"In almost every [ICP] I've worked with, [specific aspect] — and it's not the [thing] you'd guess."*

### f. Transition to solution mechanism (the next beat, not part of the 5)
Per Luis: explain the solution as the answer to the named problem.
> *"Everything you've tried before didn't work because it patched the visible symptoms. **[Solution Mechanism]** works because it addresses the [named problem] at the root."*

---

## 4. The coined-name move

The name has to be:

- **Short.** One phrase the viewer can remember after one watch.
- **Diagnostic, not insulting.** Frame the trap as structural, not a personal flaw. *"The Hidden Variable"* good. *"The Lazy Coach Trap"* bad.
- **Set up the solution.** The name should make your solution feel like the obvious next step. (Lauren's SCALE example: *the Hidden Variable* → so you need to see all 5 variables → SCALE.)
- **New to them.** Don't reuse industry-standard labels (*imposter syndrome*, *shiny-object syndrome*). Coin something.

Luis mentioned Educate.io has a **Ghost Rider** agent that helps generate this name. If you don't have it, generate 3 candidates each pulling a different lever, then pick the one that pays off your packaging:

- **Lever A** — name the *hidden cause* (e.g. "The Hidden Variable")
- **Lever B** — name the *failure mode* (e.g. "Single-Lever Scaling")
- **Lever C** — name the *broken state* (e.g. "The Broken Equation")

## 5. Worked example — Lauren's SCALE video

**Video:** *the EXACT path to $20,000/m as an online coach (no ads, no funnels)*
**ICP:** online coaches at $5-10k/month who've tried AI tools and still feel stuck. Problem-aware ✅.
**Named problem:** **the Hidden Variable.**
**Solution mechanism:** the SCALE Equation — Sales, Clients, Attention, Leads, Economics.

Teleprompter script (lowercase delivery voice — drop straight into the script as Beat 3 + Beat 4):

> *so here's what doing that for years taught me.*
>
> *most coaches think the reason they're stuck at ten grand a month is that they're not doing enough. not posting enough. not enough offers. not enough funnels. not enough hustle.*
>
> *it's the opposite. you're stuck because you're doing too much, and none of it compounds.*
>
> *here's the mechanism. a coaching business is not a hustle. it's an equation. exactly five variables. and every coach i've ever audited who is stuck has the same underlying problem. i call it **the hidden variable**.*
>
> *here's how it works. your content getting crickets, that's one variable. your AI sounding generic, same variable. your leads drying up, your pricing not holding, your launch flopping. every one of those is not a separate problem. every one is just one of the same five variables, out of place.*
>
> *so you do what every guru tells you. you work harder on the variables you can see. you post more. you tweak the offer. you DM more leads. and the number still doesn't move.*
>
> *because while you're pouring everything into the variables you can see, one of them is hidden, and it's leaking. and in almost every coaching business i've audited, it's the same one. and it's not the one you'd guess.*
>
> *that's the real problem. it isn't effort. it isn't talent. you have both. the hidden variable is what's keeping you at ten grand. find it, fix it, and the equation starts solving itself.*

Then Beat 4 (solution mechanism, explicitly framed as the answer to the named problem):

> *once you can see all five variables, the hidden one stops being hidden.*
>
> *that's what this video gives you. all five, named, in order. i call the equation **SCALE**. sales. clients. attention. leads. economics.*
>
> *every "fix" you've tried before, more content, more offers, more ads, didn't work because it was patching the variables you could see while the hidden one kept leaking. SCALE works because it shows you all five at once, so you stop guessing which one to fix and start fixing the one that actually moves the number.*

Beat 8 retention loop then closes the *"not the one you'd guess"* loop on Economics.

## 6. How to write your own variations

To make a fresh variation, you swap (a) the ICP, (b) the named problem, (c) the symptom map, (d) the solution mechanism. The scaffold stays identical. Fill the blanks:

```
ICP: [who, in one line — at what stage, with what painful situation]
Wrong belief (a): They think the reason is __________.
Reframe (b): It's actually __________.
Named problem (c): I call this __________.
Symptom map (d): [Surface symptom 1] = the [named problem]. [Symptom 2] = same. [Symptom 3] = same. [Symptom 4] = same.
Loop tease (e): In almost every [ICP], it's __________ — and it's not the one you'd guess.
Solution mechanism (f): My system is __________. Everything they tried before patched a symptom; this addresses the [named problem] at the root.
```

### Variation A — for B2B service-business owners stuck at $50k/month

- **Named problem:** *the Founder Tax.* Every dollar above $50k MRR pays a tax to the founder's calendar.
- **Reframe:** you don't have a sales problem, you have a delivery-bottleneck problem.
- **Symptom map:** late client work = the tax. missed launches = the tax. no time for sales = the tax.
- **Solution mechanism:** the productised delivery system.

### Variation B — for course creators with big audiences but flat revenue

- **Named problem:** *Audience Lag.* Big audience, small conversion, because the audience was built on a different promise than the offer.
- **Reframe:** more followers won't help, the audience-offer mismatch is the leak.
- **Symptom map:** low webinar shows = the lag. flat email opens = the lag. soft launches = the lag.
- **Solution mechanism:** the audience-offer alignment audit.

### Variation C — for agency owners earning $20-50k/month who can't scale

- **Named problem:** *the Owner Bottleneck.* Every deliverable still routes through you.
- **Reframe:** you don't have a hiring problem, you have a documented-system problem.
- **Symptom map:** sales calls only you can close = the bottleneck. clients only you can onboard = the bottleneck. content only you can write = the bottleneck.
- **Solution mechanism:** the operator-extraction system.

## 7. Quality gate — before you record

- [ ] Is the ICP **problem-aware**? (If unaware, use opportunity-first instead — don't run this scaffold.)
- [ ] Did you introduce the problem mechanism **before** the solution mechanism?
- [ ] Did you give the underlying problem a coined **name** (not just a description)?
- [ ] Did the symptom map collapse 3–5 surface problems into the named problem?
- [ ] Did you open a loop the viewer wouldn't guess, and did you avoid closing it in the same beat?
- [ ] Does the solution beat say explicitly *"everything you tried before didn't work because... this works because..."*?
- [ ] Does the coined name pay off the thumbnail and title?

---

## 8. Common failure modes

- **Describing the mechanism but not naming it.** A coined label is the difference between "interesting" and "memorable." If your draft doesn't contain *"I call this [X]"* you're not done.
- **Solution leak.** Naming the framework (SCALE, the System, the Method) inside the problem beat collapses the loop. Hold the solution name back until the transition.
- **Symptom map too generic.** "Low revenue" isn't a symptom; it's the outcome. The symptoms are the things they're currently *trying* (more content, more offers, more ads) that aren't working.
- **Problem-first on an unaware audience.** Will land flat. Switch to opportunity-first.
- **Naming the problem something the audience already calls it.** Coin something new — that's the point of the move.

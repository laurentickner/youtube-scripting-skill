## ⭐ THE CALLAWAY 7-BEAT SKELETON (EMPIRICAL, ≥50% HIT RATE, LOCKED 2026-06-05)

Re-audited 2026-06-05 across 51 body points in Callaway's 10 transcripts. These are the beats Callaway actually uses ≥50% of the time. Apply this skeleton FIRST to every body point. The expanded 12-step list below is reference detail.

```
1. NAME      "Alright, [Nth] thing is called [TERM-BRANDED CONCEPT]."     (94%)
2. DEFINE    "[1-2 sentences. What it is, plain.]"                        (88%)
3. CONTRAST  "Most coaches do X. What actually works is Y."               (56%)
4. WHY       "Here's why this works. [psychology/math/system logic]"      (86%)
5. EXAMPLE   "[Named person/case] did this. They got [specific result]."  (76%)
6. TACTIC    "Here's the tactic. [Specific action this week.]"            (82%)
7. BRIDGE    "Alright, that's the [Nth] thing. The next one is…"          (78%)
```

**Signal phrases (Callaway's actual words):**
- NAME: "Alright, [first/next] thing is called…" / "I call this…"
- WHY: "Here's why this works." / "The reason this is so powerful is…"
- EXAMPLE: "For example…" / "[Person] did this." / "Watch this."
- TACTIC: "Here's the tactic." / "Here's how you can apply this today."
- BRIDGE: "Alright, [the next/second] thing is…" / "Okay, that was [X]. Now…"

**Below-50% beats — USE SPARINGLY, only if they fit the specific BP:**
Zoom-out (46%), Rehook (38%), Personal story (30%), Analogy (28%), Sub-mechanism split (24%), Objection handler (22%), Rule refrain (18%), Confession (12%).

**Lauren-specific calibration:**
- **CUT** rule refrain repetition (you do 8-10x, Callaway does 1-2x)
- **CUT** per-BP video-level zoom-outs (Callaway reserves these for outro)
- **ADD** sharp CONTRAST frame at beat 3 ("most coaches think X, actually Y")
- **ADD** at least one NAMED student/client example per body point (52% Callaway)
- **KEEP** explicit TACTIC at close (82% Callaway, non-negotiable)

## The Packaging Sequence (every body point follows this)

### 1. OPENER — Name it
- "All right, the first thing..." OR "So the first thing..." (Lauren's voice variation OK)
- Just name the term-branded mechanism. No preview list.
- One sentence.

### 2. DEFINE — Say what it is, plainly
- Immediately after the name. Same paragraph.
- "[Mechanism] is this. [One-sentence definition that includes the math/structure]."
- Kyle: this is the STATE pillar landing.

### 3. ANALOGY — Make it tangible (CRITICAL)
- Right after the definition.
- One-paragraph analogy from a domain the viewer already knows (realtors, investments, doctors, contractors).
- This is what makes the abstract concept stick.
- Callaway does this in 8/10 body points.

### 4. WHY IT WORKS — Pull forward
- "And here's why this works."
- Explain the underlying mechanism in 1-2 sentences.
- Callaway: 10/10 of his body points have the why BEFORE any example.

### 5. PERSONAL STORY (when applicable) — Lauren-strategic
- If Lauren has a personal story that matches the viewer's situation, drop it here as the FIRST proof.
- Callaway puts personal stories after famous-anchor cites. Lauren doesn't need a famous anchor because she IS the authority for coaches.
- The story plays the role of the "famous-anchor cite" for non-creator audiences who don't know coaching-niche names.

### 6. EXAMPLES — 3 max, ICP-matched
- Cap at 3 examples. Callaway's empirical average is 2.8.
- Interleave the audience's likely niches (financial AND emotional/health for coaches).
- Don't do fragment lists ("A confidence client. A parenting client. A spiritual client."). Pick one and develop it.

### 7. OBJECTION HANDLER — In viewer's voice
- "If you're sitting there going 'but [their objection],' that's exactly what I thought when..."
- Callaway uses this in 6/10 body points.
- Always neutralise immediately. One sentence fix.

### 8. CONFESSION (if Lauren has one) — Teaching beat, not regret-flex
- Frame as "took me X years to figure this out, cost me Y" not "I left X on the table."
- Per Callaway empirical pattern: confessions land mid-body, not in opener.

### 9. RULE REFRAIN — Quotable, repeated
- One quotable line that compresses the whole teach into 5-7 words.
- Examples: "Price the outcome, not the work" / "Filter for the right fit, not the right wallet"
- Lands 2-3 times in the section.

### 10. TACTIC BEAT — "Here's how to apply this today"
- Concrete action the viewer can take in the next hour.
- One question to ask their next prospect, or one rewrite to their bio.
- Callaway closes 10/10 body points with a tactic.

### 11. FRAMING — ⚠️ EMPIRICALLY RARE — DEFAULT OFF
- **Audited 2026-06-05 against Callaway's 10 transcripts. Per-BP framing zoom-out present in only 18% (9/51) of body points.**
- The "Value Loop" spec (Context → Application → Framing) is NOT Callaway's real pattern.
- Callaway's real per-BP pattern is: **Context → Why-it-works (mechanism/psychology) → Application + examples**.
- The video-level zoom-out ("how this matters for the whole video") lives in the OUTRO, not per BP.
- **Use per-BP framing ONLY when each body point is literally a rung of an escalating ladder** (e.g. levels 1/2/3 of an obsession system). Otherwise drop it.
- Hard rule: zero "and here's why this matters for everything else in this video" inside a body point. Save it for outro recap.
- The "outro meta-stake" pattern Callaway uses ("this is the cake, everything else is icing" / "this will be the best channel you can watch") IS the framing — it lives at video close, not BP close.

### 12. TRANSITION — Closer + Opener
- Pattern: "So that's [BP1 distilled to 5-7 words]. All right, the second thing they understand is [next mechanism named]."
- Add a tease: "...because [this rule/this filter/this process] only works if [hook for next point]."
- NEVER use "Which brings us to" — 0 instances in Callaway's 10 transcripts.

## Banned Across Both Frameworks

Hard-banned phrases (never use):
- "Here's the thing" (used too often becomes AI tell)
- "The truth is"
- "Look,"
- "Listen,"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Which brings us to" (zero Callaway instances)
- Em-dashes (Lauren's locked voice rule)
- Atmospheric adverbs (quietly, subtly, actively, actually as filler)
- AI-shape triples (3 perfectly weighted parallel clauses)
- Forced punchy fragments ("A confidence client. A parenting client. A spiritual client.")

## Audience-Specific Adaptations for Lauren

**Lauren's ICP is coaches**, including non-B2B coaches (fitness, life, dating, parenting, confidence, spiritual). This is different from Callaway's audience (content creators).

Key adaptations:
1. **Personal story PLACEMENT** — Lauren's personal stories work as first proof point, not as opener. Callaway puts famous-anchor cites first; Lauren doesn't have Hormozi-equivalents for non-B2B coaches, so her own story plays that role.
2. **Example mix** — Always include at least one non-financial example in the first 2 examples. Pure financial examples (salary, surgery) make non-B2B coaches think "not for me" early.
3. **No famous-anchor name-drops** unless they're universally known (e.g., realtors, Tesla, surgeons — universal references, not creator-niche references).

## The Body Point Packaging Checklist

Before shipping any body point script:

- [ ] Opener names the mechanism in one sentence
- [ ] Definition lands in the very next sentence
- [ ] One-paragraph analogy makes the concept tangible
- [ ] "And here's why this works" pulled BEFORE first example
- [ ] Personal story (if used) lands as first proof, not opener
- [ ] Maximum 3 examples, ICP-matched (mix financial + emotional)
- [ ] Objection handler in viewer's voice ("if you're sitting there going...")
- [ ] Confession reframed as teaching beat, not regret-flex
- [ ] Quotable RULE refrain lands 2-3 times
- [ ] Tactic beat: "Here's how to apply this today"
- [ ] NO per-BP framing zoom-out unless this BP is a literal ladder-rung (default OFF — Callaway only does this 18% of the time)
- [ ] Closer-opener transition: "So that's [BP1 distilled]. All right, the second thing..."
- [ ] Open-loop tease into next body point
- [ ] NO mid-body share/comment CTAs (save for outro)
- [ ] NO "Which brings us to"
- [ ] NO em-dashes
- [ ] NO banned bridges or atmospheric adverbs
- [ ] Word count: 500-800 words = 3-5 min spoken
- [ ] Pattern coverage score: 9/10 on both Callaway and Kyle

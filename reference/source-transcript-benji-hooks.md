## Distilled rule — "Don't Give It Away"

**The hook opens the loop; the body pays it off. The hook line itself must NOT contain the
mechanism/explanation.**

1. **State the problem or a contrarian claim, withhold the answer.** "…has nothing to do with posting
   more" creates tension without telling the viewer what it IS about. That is the curiosity gap.
2. **The failure mode:** a hook line that IS the mechanism ("every post teaches the platform who to
   show you to"). It can be a *true, sharp* line and still be the wrong move — it closes the loop, the
   viewer thinks "oh, I get it," and leaves.
3. **If you must reference the mechanism, hide it behind an unexplained named container** — a new
   tool / method / branded name ("this new method," "the [Name] system") so *novelty* drives curiosity
   without resolving it. Name it, don't explain it. (Same logic as the acronym-packaging rule: never
   spell out what's inside a container in the beat that introduces it.)
4. **Two levers keep the viewer past the hook: authority + open curiosity.** The authority beat
   ("helped hundreds of 7–9 figure businesses…") isn't just credibility — it's what earns the right to
   be believed when the mechanism finally lands. Give the mechanism away up front and the viewer has
   to accept a bare claim from someone they don't know yet (weak, easy to dismiss). Withhold it, prove
   authority first, and the reveal in the body lands on earned trust.
